Amiodarone shared care: information for patients

Information and guidance for patients and carers in England

Dear Patient prescribed Amiodarone,

You have been prescribed Amiodarone by the Cardiology Department, Derriford Hospital. This is a shared care medication, which means you must read, agree to, and complete the attached questionnaire before we can prescribe medicines safely. Once we have received and processed this contract, we will contact you to let you know when we will take over prescribing and monitoring at Beacon Medical Group. Until then, please attend the hospital as normal for tests and request prescriptions from the Cardiology Department, Derriford Hospital.

As with any drugs please ensure you have familiarised yourself with the manufacturer’s printed information leaflet and that you take this medication exactly as directed by your clinician.

While you are taking Amiodarone you may become more sensitive to the harmful effects of sunlight, avoid sitting out in the sun and use a sun cream of factor 30 or higher. Take amiodarone as directed by the prescriber. Patients wishing to reduce their dose or stop amiodarone should discuss with their specialist before doing so. Avoid abrupt withdrawal unless advised by the GP, Pharmacist or specialist.

You should ensure that any clinician prescribing for you knows that you are taking Amiodarone. Likewise you should check with your pharmacist before taking any over-the-counter medicines or homeopathic medicines. If you are having any treatment like an operation or dental treatment, tell the person carrying out the treatment what medication you are taking.

Most people develop corneal microdeposits in the eye during treatment. Corneal microdeposits may be associated with blurred vision. Be aware of the effects on driving and performance of skilled tasks. Drivers may be dazzled by headlights at night. Eye examination is required if you get visual symptoms; however due to the potential for micro deposits to occur regular optician visits are encouraged. Inform optometrists that you take amiodarone.

Avoid grapefruit juice while taking amiodarone and for several months after discontinuation. Moderate alcohol intake to no more than 14 units per week to reduce the risk of liver damage.

If you are taking a statin and amiodarone, report any signs of unexplained muscle pain, tenderness, weakness or dark coloured urine to your GP surgery or specialist.

It is important to report the following signs and symptoms to the GP or hospital straight away during treatment or in the 3 month period after stopping amiodarone:

  • Breathlessness, non-productive cough or deterioration in general health
  • New or worsening visual disturbances, including loss of eyesight, blurred or decreased vision
  • Yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice), feeling tired or sick, loss of appetite, stomach pain, high temperature
  • Weakness, weight loss or weight gain, heat or cold intolerance, hair thinning, sweating, changes in menstrual periods, swelling of the neck (goitre), nervousness, irritability, restlessness, or decreased concentration
  • Progressive skin rash +/- blisters or mucosal lesions
  • Signs and symptoms of fast heart rate or other heart problems, e.g. dizziness, fatigue, fainting, shortness of breath, chest pain or palpitations, confusion or trouble concentrating
  • Uneven or erratic heartbeat or heartbeat becomes very slow

Your Pharmacy should provide you with a patient alert card, or a digital version can be found here https://bit.ly/AmiodaroneAlert

The practice has a large number of patients on Amiodarone so it is not possible for us to initiate routine contact with each person to arrange the blood test; that responsibility has to rest with you. So, a week before your blood test is due you should contact reception at your normal surgery (after 11 am when they are less busy) to arrange this appointment and, two days after your appointment, to obtain the results.

Regular physical health and blood test monitoring is required with this medication. You will need Liver Function Blood Tests, Kidney Function Blood tests (Including Magnesium and potassium), and Thyroid Function Blood tests at least every 6 months whilst using this medication. You will also need an ECG at least once every 12 months.

It is important that this monitoring is done for your safety. If the monitoring is not done as laid out in the shared care agreement (See https://bmg.link/Amiodarone for details), we will have to reduce the quantity prescribed until the monitoring is up to date. If this continues to happen this will breach the terms of the Shared Care Agreement, and we will have to hand back care and prescribing responsibility to the hospital who started the medication.

If the hospital advise you to change your dose they should arrange with you the necessary monitoring as per the shared care agreement.

If you are housebound we will arrange for a member of the community nursing team to take your blood sample but the onus will still be on you, or your representative, to contact the surgery to request this and, after two working days after the test, to obtain your result. If a member of the community nursing team does not attend to take your blood sample on the expected date, please notify reception at your normal surgery.

Attached to this information, you will have received a questionnaire which you will need to complete to acknowledge you have read the above and understand that if you do not comply with the above we will have to end the shared care agreement.

If you have any further questions please complete an online consultation here https://bmg.link/ or contact the surgery on 01752 346634 to arrange an appointment to discuss.
